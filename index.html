Working Memory Lab

Do the dual-task experiments yourself. Predict what will happen, run the conditions, then compare your results with the original studies.

Study 1: the visuo-spatial sketchpad

Aim
To investigate whether visual and spatial tasks use the same system within working memory, the visuo-spatial sketchpad.
Method
Task 1: mentally trace the shape of the letter F and say “yes” or “no” depending on whether each corner is at the top or bottom.
Task 2: either a visual task (track a moving light with a pointer) or a verbal task (say “yes” repeatedly out loud).

Step 1 · Predict

Which pairing do you think will be harder for you?

Step 2 · Run the four conditions

Do them in order, 1 to 4. Use a mouse or a finger to track the light. On a keyboard, press Y or N to answer.

Step 3 · Your results

Study 2: the phonological loop

Aim
To test if working memory has separate components by seeing if people can do two tasks at once without performance suffering.
Method
Participants did two tasks at the same time:
A reasoning task (for example “B follows A – AB?”).
A digit span task, where they had to remember 1 to 6 digits.

Step 1 · Predict

What will happen to your reasoning when you also hold 6 digits in mind?

Step 2 · Run the four rounds

In each round you see some digits, then answer 6 reasoning questions while you keep the digits in mind, then type the digits back. Say them over and over (out loud or in your head). On a keyboard, press T for true and F for false.

Step 3 · Your results

Check yourself

Pick an answer to see why it is right or wrong.
